Evidence

Evidence that someone else can check.

Each decision and each release leaves a signed record. An auditor, regulator or customer can verify it without access to your systems and without trusting Parmana.

What a record contains

The request

What the agent asked to do, with its exact parameters and amount.

The decision

Whether the action was authorized, which rule applied, and any approval that was required and given.

The release

What actually ran, or the refusal if nothing ran. Each record is hashed and signed.

How verification works

Verification runs four checks. All four always run, and a single report names every check that fails, not just the first.

1

Integrity

The record's content still matches its recorded hash.

2

Signature

The signature verifies against the public key the record names.

3

Authorization binding

Every approved execution carries the authorization it relied on. Refusals are recorded as refusals.

4

Chain integrity

Chained records link to their predecessor, so changing or removing one is detectable. Records written before chaining began are not covered.

Check a record yourself

You do not need to install Parmana to verify a record. You need the record and the public key you trust.

Verification documentation
Verification shows that a record is intact and was signed by the key it names. It does not show that the key's owner is trustworthy, and it cannot prove an action happened outside the gateway. Key management is your responsibility. Parmana's key custody is local files or AWS KMS today.
