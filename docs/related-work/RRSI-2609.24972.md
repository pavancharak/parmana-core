- **Code:** [github.com/google-research/rrsi](https://github.com/google-research/rrsi), site

- **Noted:** 2026-10-11

## What it says

An agent is a frozen model plus a **harness**: prompts, control flow, tools, memory and context management. Recent
systems let an LLM rewrite the harness from task feedback, a form of recursive self-improvement. Because the same finite
task set is reused round after round, the search overfits: benchmark-specific tricks, chasing evaluation noise, and
growing complexity raise the tuning score without transferring to new tasks.

RRSI keeps the whole harness editable but regularizes the search:

- **Proposal side:** an edit budget per round that shrinks over time (L0-style); credit assigned over the whole history
  of gains and regressions; exploration steered to untried components when progress stalls.
- **Selection side:** a critic rejects benchmark- or task-specific logic (leakage screening); a gain must exceed
  evaluation noise; extra cost must be paid for by measured gain (L2-style); components with no recent contribution are
  pruned (L1-style).

## Results (as reported)

- Eight benchmarks in coding, agentic workspace and engineering design; evolve on one suite, run unchanged on the others.
- Up to +6.0 points on the evolve split and up to +4.3 on the five out-of-distribution benchmarks; the evolved harness
  uses about 30% fewer policy tokens than unregularized evolution (2.42 vs 3.80 million per trial on agentic workspace).
- Unregularized evolution scored highest on the evolve split (92.8) but stayed within a point of the unevolved harness
  out of distribution (40.3 vs 39.7); RRSI scored 43.6.
- Gains held across two model families (Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.5 Flash) and transferred to a weaker model never used
  in the search.

**Stated limitations:** frozen model weights only; depends on a finite evolve set, the quality of its feedback signal
and several hyperparameters; broader validation needed for other architectures and longer-running self-improvement.

## What it means for Parmana

1. **The agent's own guardrails are part of what gets rewritten.** RRSI's edit space deliberately includes prompts,
   control flow, tools, skills, memory and subagents, and its leakage critic is itself an LLM step in the loop. A rule
   that lives inside the harness is a candidate edit. Parmana's checks sit outside it: the policy, authority grants,
   business signal sources and approver keys change only through maker checker with a human step-up signature, and
   an agent's key cannot propose or approve those changes.
2. **"Better at the task" is not "allowed to do more".** A self-improving agent can get faster or more accurate; under
   Parmana its authority (capabilities, grant limits, required approvals) does not move with it. Useful line:
   _agents will rewrite themselves; their authority should not move when they do._
3. **Reward hacking has a business-fact equivalent.** RRSI shows search exploiting whatever the score measures. An agent
   optimized on outcomes could learn to assert the facts that get actions approved; business validation (RFC-0023)
   asks the owning system instead of trusting the agent's claim.
4. **Records survive harness changes.** Each decision's signed record names the policy version and the facts it
   rested on, so behavior can be compared before and after an agent's harness evolves.

Possible follow-ups (not scheduled): a docs-site note on self-improving agents; recording the harness version an
agent ran under as a request field, so records can be grouped by harness.
